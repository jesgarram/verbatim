# Why I Stopped Using Frameworks

In today's rapidly evolving landscape of web development, there are many choices to make. Frameworks come and go, and it's important to stay up to date.

## The Problem

We built this system because we needed something better. The old system wasn't working. So we decided to build a new one that would solve our problems.

The thing is, I realized that most frameworks are basically just adding complexity on top of complexity. You end up spending more time configuring the framework than actually building things.

## What I Did Instead

I went back to basics. Plain HTML, CSS, and a little bit of JavaScript. This improved our workflow significantly. The team was happier, the code was simpler, and we shipped faster.

It's worth noting that this approach isn't for everyone. Some teams genuinely need the structure that a framework provides. But for our use case, going frameworkless was the right call.

## The Results

After making the switch, everything just clicked. We reduced our build times, simplified our deployment pipeline, and honestly, the code was just easier to reason about. I think that's something a lot of developers overlook when they're choosing tools.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, I believe that the best tool is the one that gets out of your way. Frameworks are great, but they're not always necessary. Sometimes the simplest approach is the most powerful one. It's really about understanding your needs and making thoughtful decisions about your tech stack.
