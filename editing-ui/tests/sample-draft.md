# Why I Built This Tool

The thing about building tools is that you never really know what you need until you're deep in the process. I started this project because I was tired of the same loop — give a talk, transcribe it, then spend hours trying to turn that transcript into something readable.

And the weird part? The more I edited, the less it sounded like me. Every pass through the text made it a little more *polished* and a little less **authentic**. That's the trap.

## The First Attempt

I tried a few things before landing on this approach. The first version was basically just "throw the transcript at Claude and ask for a blog post." The output was fine — technically correct, well-structured — but it wasn't *me*. It read like a thousand other AI-generated blog posts.

So I flipped the model. Instead of asking the AI to write, I asked it to edit. Big difference.

## What Makes This Different

`Here's what I learned: the speaker's voice is the product. Not the structure, not the grammar, not the "flow." The voice. When someone reads your blog post and thinks "yeah, that sounds like something they'd say" — that's the goal.`

Three principles guide the whole thing:

1. Every word traces back to something actually said

2. The voice profile is the constitution — it overrides everything else

3. If it smells like AI wrote it, cut it

> The best writing tools are invisible. They make the writer's voice clearer, not replace it with something "better."

## What's Next

I'm still iterating. The pipeline works well for the initial draft, but the editing loop after that — the back-and-forth of "change this paragraph" and "make that tighter" — is still too slow. That's the next thing to fix.

`The dream is a visual editor where I can just point at a paragraph and say what I want changed. We'll see.`
