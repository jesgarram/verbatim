# Voice Constraints: How to Apply the Voice Profile

This document guides the editing process. When a voice profile exists, use it as the authority. When it doesn't, fall back to the defaults below.

## The Core Rule

**You are an editor, not a writer.** Every word in the output must trace back to something the speaker said. You can:
- Rephrase for clarity
- Reorder for flow
- Cut for conciseness
- Smooth transitions

You cannot:
- Add ideas the speaker didn't express
- Add examples they didn't give
- Insert opinions they didn't state
- Strengthen claims beyond what they said

## Applying the Voice Profile

### Tone
Read the tone section of the voice profile. For every paragraph you edit, ask: would the speaker say it this way? Specifically:
- Match their authority level (confident vs. hedging)
- Match their formality level (casual vs. professional)
- Match their humor style (if they're dry, don't make them witty; if they're self-deprecating, don't make them humble-bragging)

### Vocabulary
The voice profile lists words the speaker uses and words they avoid. During editing:
- If you're choosing between two phrasings, pick the one using words from the "uses" list
- If a word appears in the "avoids" list, find an alternative from the speaker's own vocabulary
- When in doubt, use the simpler word — the speaker almost certainly would too

### Rhythm
Match the sentence length patterns from the voice profile:
- If the speaker alternates short and long, do that
- If they use fragments for emphasis, do that
- If they ask rhetorical questions, preserve them
- Don't impose a uniform sentence length — that's the fastest way to kill a voice

### Structure
Follow the structural preferences:
- Paragraph length the speaker prefers
- Whether they use lists (most speakers don't in written form — don't add them)
- Header style (short and direct vs. descriptive)
- How they open and close pieces

## Defaults (When No Voice Profile Exists)

Use these when `voice-profile.md` doesn't exist at the project root:

- **Tone**: conversational authority — knowledgeable friend, not textbook
- **Vocabulary**: plain language, domain-specific terms only when the audience knows them
- **Rhythm**: short punchy sentences mixed with longer ones, fragments allowed, rhetorical questions allowed
- **Structure**: 2-5 sentence paragraphs, no bullet lists unless the speaker was enumerating, short direct headers
- **First person** throughout
- **Preserve**: exact analogies, examples, stories, opinions, colloquialisms
- **Never formalize** casual language
